# GTT 0.0.70 — Native Road Body Damage, Detachable Panels & Workshop Integration

## Added

- Native Rattleback 82 and Mulebox 1200 now track four independent body-damage zones: FRONT, REAR, LEFT and RIGHT.
- Native collision hits are classified from the real hit location, then apply both shared vehicle condition damage and local zone damage.
- Each zone can shed a lightweight runtime debris panel after a sufficiently hard impact. Detached pieces use physics and impact impulse instead of remaining cosmetic counters.
- Front-end damage now creates cooling stress under sustained throttle. Cooling stress progressively reduces available power and can slowly worsen overall condition when the player keeps pushing a badly damaged front end.
- Left/right damage reduces steering authority and introduces a small directional pull when one side is materially worse than the other.
- Rear damage and missing body panels now contribute to Native wheel/traction risk, connecting crash damage to the existing Chaos wheel-state controller.
- Added `NATIVE_ROAD_DAMAGE_ZONE`, `NATIVE_ROAD_PANEL_DETACH`, `NATIVE_ROAD_DAMAGE_DYNAMICS` and `NATIVE_ROAD_WORKSHOP_RESTORE` runtime evidence.

## Workshop / economy integration

- The existing workshop now detects an active Native Rattleback/Mulebox before touching its hidden legacy mirror.
- Native road workshop service repairs condition, tires, body zones and cooling stress, restores detached debris state, refuels the vehicle and immediately synchronizes the compatibility mirror.
- Body damage adds a bounded repair surcharge based on average zone damage, detached panel count and cooling stress; fuel-only/light service stays near the base workshop price.
- Generic legacy workshop scanning skips Rattleback/Mulebox mirrors while their Native takeover is active, preventing stale-snapshot repair behavior.

## Known limitation / Demo status

This milestone is source/runtime implementation and CI-contract work. The runtime debris pieces deliberately use an original engine primitive fallback so the mechanic exists without protected third-party assets; authored vehicle-specific damaged-body meshes can replace them later. This is not proof of a compiled Unreal Engine 5.8 Win64 package. Demo remains blocked until a real Win64 package is built, the packaged executable passes runtime smoke testing, rendered visual acceptance passes, relevant CI is green, and no demo-critical blocker remains.
